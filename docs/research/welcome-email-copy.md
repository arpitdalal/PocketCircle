# Welcome email copy research

Researched 2026-09-07. Scope: first-party welcome and onboarding patterns that can inform PocketCircle's signup email. No application changes.

## Evidence

| Source | What it establishes | Application to PocketCircle |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [Todoist welcome-kit guide](https://www.todoist.com/toolkit/set-up-your-team-workspace/meet-them-at-the-door) | Todoist advises teams to explain why the product helps, give specific first tasks, link directly to projects, and name a help channel. It explicitly discourages vague directions to explore the app or set up a profile. This is onboarding guidance, not Todoist's own signup email. | Explain what a Circle is for. Give a useful action and a working link. Profile completion should not be the main benefit. |
| [Buffer's account of its automated welcome](https://buffer.com/resources/marketing-automation/) | Buffer's October 2015 article includes a screenshot identified as its then-current automated welcome email. It describes founder outreach and recommends names, friendly language, and introducing the sender. Historical evidence, not confirmation of today's email. | A short note from Arpit is a credible alternative to a generic team greeting. Include an invitation to reply only if someone receives and answers replies. |
| [YNAB getting-started guide](https://www.ynab.com/guide/the-ultimate-get-started-guide) | YNAB organizes onboarding around concrete financial actions: build categories, collect cash, and assign money. It states the setup requires about 20-30 minutes. This is an onboarding guide, not a welcome email. | Name something a user can actually do with their spending. Do not promise instant results or a setup time without verifying the app's workflow. |
| [Customer.io welcome email copy recipe](https://docs.customer.io/messaging/channels/email/recipes/welcome-email-copy/) | The email platform's own general-product template combines a greeting, product purpose, a useful next step with its benefit, a CTA, and a help channel. Its example app is fictional. | Keep the email brief, explain the value, and add one primary link back to PocketCircle. This is a writing pattern, not evidence of a measured conversion lift. |

## Recommendation

These are editorial recommendations inferred from the sources above, not tested performance claims.

- Keep the friendly greeting and existing welcome subject.
- Replace generic shared-spending language with a clear explanation of personal and shared use.
- Give one primary CTA, such as `Open PocketCircle`, linked to the app. Specific action labels are preferable only when their destination supports that action for every recipient.
- Avoid directing everyone to finish their profile. The welcome should remain useful if they already completed setup.
- For a more personal version, use a real sender and a real reply channel. Otherwise retain the team signature.
- Do not claim other products currently send the exact wording above. Only Buffer supplies an actual historical welcome example; the other sources supply onboarding or email-writing guidance.

## Copy directions

1. Clear default: welcome, explain personal versus shared Circles, open the app.
2. Action-led: suggest starting with an everyday expense, then open the app. Check category/setup prerequisites before promising direct expense entry.
3. Personal note: brief welcome from Arpit, product purpose, app link. Add a reply invitation only after confirming support routing.
