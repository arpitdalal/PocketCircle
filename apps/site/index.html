What a Circle is

A Circle is a shared financial space with one or more Members. It can be a residence, a trip, a family, or any budget you split. Your Personal Circle is created when you sign in and stays yours alone. You make a second Circle when you want someone else in the numbers.

One ledger instead of the group chat

Four things that make a shared money conversation short.

  • Everyone writes to the same list

    Every expense and every payment lands in one place with a title, an amount, a date, and Categories. Nobody has to scroll back through a chat to find out what the electric bill came to.

  • Totals that answer the question

    Every Circle has a Dashboard for any month: income, expenses, net, a breakdown by Category, and how this month compares with the ones before it.

  • Attribution on every row

    Each Transaction remembers who recorded it and whose money it was. Who paid for the internet has an answer that does not depend on anyone's memory.

  • A record of every change

    Circle History and Transaction History keep who changed what and when. When a shared cost is questioned months later, the Circle explains itself.

Let an assistant read the ledger

PocketCircle speaks MCP, the protocol ChatGPT, Claude, Codex, and Cursor already use. Connect an assistant, pick the Circles you want it to have in the browser, and it can read those Circles' Transactions and record new ones. It cannot see a Circle you did not pick, and it does learn who you are — your Display Name and profile picture, which is how it tells your own Transactions from everyone else's. It never sees your Google account, and you can revoke any connection from Connections at any time.

Up and running in minutes

  1. Step 1

    Sign in with Google

    One click, and there is no password to remember. We take Google's account identifier, your email address, your name, and your profile picture, and Privacy Policy lists exactly what that is. You confirm your name once to get started.

  2. Step 2

    Open a Circle

    Your Personal Circle is there from the start, solo and private. When you want someone else in the numbers, create a shared Circle: name it, give it a colour and a currency, and answer the setup questions, which seed the Categories it starts with.

  3. Step 3

    Invite, then record

    From a shared Circle, add Members by email. Each Invitation is one link that expires in seven days, and once it is accepted the list is shared.

Straight answers

What PocketCircle does, and what it does not do yet.

Does PocketCircle work out who owes whom?

No. Settlement is not built. PocketCircle records what happened and reports the totals, which is the part that is hard to keep straight by hand. Working out the split is a conversation you have with the ledger in front of you.

Is there a free plan, or a trial that expires?

There is no paid plan to upgrade to and no trial to lose, so there is nothing to decide and nothing that lapses. Sign in and the app is yours to use.

Can I use it on my own?

Yes. Your Personal Circle is created when you sign in and stays solo. It cannot invite Members, be archived, or be deleted outside Account Deletion, so it is there whenever you want to write something down without asking anyone. It starts with no Categories, and a Transaction needs at least one, so your first entry begins by making one.

Who can see a Circle I am in?

Only the Members in it. Circle names and email addresses are not searchable, so nobody can find a Circle they are not in. Three things worth knowing beyond that. Holding an Invitation Link shows you the Circle's name, the Owner's name and picture, and the email it was sent to — that is how you know whether to accept it, and you see it whether or not you do. Circle History is the Circle's own record, so if you own it, the addresses invitations went to are in there, and an assistant reading it can read them too. And an assistant you connected yourself reads the Circles you picked for it; revoking stops it asking again, but anything it has already read is in its hands, not ours.

What happens when someone leaves a Circle?

A Member can leave a Circle themselves, and the Owner can remove one. Either way their Transactions stay in the Circle, still attributed to them, and Circle History keeps the record of the change. If the same person is invited back later, their old rows resolve to their current name and picture again.

Can it import transactions from my bank?

Not yet. Transactions are entered by hand, and an assistant connected over MCP can add them for you from a message you send it. You can export a Circle's Transactions as a CSV whenever you want one.

Can I set a budget or a spending limit?

No. PocketCircle records what actually happened, not what you meant to spend, and it does not tell you whether you are over. The Dashboard's month over month comparison is the closest thing to a warning it has.

Does it work on a phone?

Yes. PocketCircle installs to your home screen and works on any device you sign in from, and it can send push notifications for Invitations and the other events in your Notification Center.

Can I delete my account?

Yes, from Settings, and it is permanent. Before it will go, PocketCircle asks you to put the Circles you are holding in order: hand over any Circle that has other Members in it, and archive any Circle you are alone in that has already been set up. One you are alone in and never finished setting up is not a blocker, and goes with you. Your login, your profile, and your notifications then go. Transactions you recorded stay in the Circles that remain, with your name still on them, because deleting your account should not quietly delete someone else's rent history.
